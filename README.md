I started where most product managers don't - knee-deep in **ETL pipelines**. At Tata Consultancy Services and then KPMG, I spent two years moving data through enterprise systems for Fortune 500 retail clients and advising 15+ companies on go-to-market expansion. The work taught me a habit I've never lost: when a product feels broken, look at the data shape first. Most product problems are data-shape problems wearing a UX costume.

I crossed into product at **Filo Edtech** in 2022, and the first thing they handed me was a marketplace launch. We took the US tutoring side **from 0 to 120K users and $1.5M ARR in six months**, then scaled the global platform to **3.3M+ users at 250K DAU**. I rejected the easy lift-and-shift of India's matching logic - it overfit to a different market - and built a US-specific demand-supply layer instead. Two-sided marketplaces taught me a second habit: growth at speed isn't a marketing problem. It's a systems problem.

Then GPT-3.5 dropped, and I got curious. At **Supreme Components International** in Singapore, I shipped one of the company's first production AI workflows - a closed-loop sales pipeline with classification guardrails and human-in-the-loop review. Quote response time dropped from 18 hours to under 1 hour. That was the moment I realized the interesting frontier wasn't the model itself. It was the **trust scaffolding around the model**.

So I took the founder leap. **Closphere** was an ERP-agnostic intelligence layer for Indian SMBs - sitting on top of Tally, Zoho, Marg, and Excel, giving small businesses inventory intelligence without forcing a workflow migration. We grew to **63 paying customers** across 100+ organizations, reduced stockouts 32%, and eventually closed with a **successful technology sale** to an enterprise inbound. I came out of it with the conviction that now ships every product I touch: people don't adopt capability. They adopt trust.

I went back into product to apply that lesson at scale. At **ProductSquads Technolabs** in 2025, I led a configurable GenAI document intelligence platform processing **100K+ documents per month at 95%+ accuracy**. The breakthrough wasn't a better model - it was a confidence-threshold pipeline (>0.90 auto-pass, 0.65–0.90 reviewer queue, <0.65 isolated) and a 12-category exception taxonomy that turned reviewer feedback into schema-level quality loops. Turnaround time dropped 70%.

Now I'm at the **University of Washington**, completing my MS in Information Management with a Product & AI specialization (graduating August 2026). I'm using the time to formalize the AI/ML foundation underneath everything I've already built - agentic systems, LLM evals, applied machine learning, and the harder stuff that doesn't show up in production until you really need it.

## How I think about product

- **Trust beats capability.** The hard part of AI products isn't the model - it's giving users enough signal, control, and reversibility that they'll actually depend on the output. Most AI products fail at this layer, not the technical one.
- **Prototype before PRD.** If I can't build a janky version, I haven't understood the problem yet. The PRD comes after the prototype, not before it.
- **Systems over symptoms.** Most product problems are surface signals of structural choices made earlier. The fix is usually upstream of where the pain is felt.
